Clothes made after you order them.

Loomwell is an example direct-to-consumer clothing label. It sells small-batch basics in limited drops, so nothing is made on a guess and almost nothing goes unsold.

Illustrative business model

The business model canvas

Nine blocks that show how Loomwell creates, delivers and earns from its clothing.

Key partners

  • Small cut-and-sew factories in Portugal
  • Organic cotton and recycled-fibre mills
  • Courier and returns partners
  • Local tailors for repairs

Key activities

  • Designing 2 drops a year
  • Pre-order campaigns
  • Batch production planning
  • Content and community

Key resources

  • Design team and pattern library
  • Supplier relationships
  • Online store and customer data
  • Brand and social following

Value proposition

  • Well-fitting, durable basics at fair prices
  • Made in small batches, so less waste
  • Free repairs for two years
  • Transparent costs on every product page

Customer relationships

  • Early access for members
  • Repair and resale service
  • Fit advice by chat

Channels

  • Own website (main channel)
  • Instagram and TikTok
  • Pop-up shops and markets
  • Email drops

Customer segments

  • Students and young professionals, 18–35
  • Shoppers who care about sustainability
  • Buyers who want fewer, better clothes

Cost structure

  • Fabric, trims and manufacturing (about 45% of price)
  • Shipping, packaging and returns
  • Marketing and website costs
  • Salaries and studio overheads

Revenue streams

  • Direct online sales of drops
  • Membership (£25 a year) for early access and repairs
  • Pre-loved resale commission
  • Wholesale to a few select shops
